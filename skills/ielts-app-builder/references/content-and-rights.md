# Content and rights

Use this reference before importing IELTS questions, audio, vocabulary, books, samples, model answers, or scoring language.

## Separate format, provenance, and permission

These are different claims:

- “IELTS-style” describes a format.
- “Official public sample” identifies material released by an IELTS owner or partner.
- “Past paper” claims the material appeared in a real examination.
- “Cambridge original paper” identifies commercially copyrighted book content.

Never infer the stronger claim from the weaker one.

## Safe content paths

Prefer one of these:

1. Original content written for the app and reviewed against public IELTS format guidance.
2. Official public sample material linked or embedded under its published terms, with the exact source and no implication of ownership.
3. Licensed commercial content with recorded scope, territory, duration, and redistribution rights.
4. Open or permissively licensed language data with attribution and change notices.

Do not copy Cambridge books, commercial question banks, recordings, answer keys, or proprietary vocabulary lists into a product without permission. Renaming copied material or omitting the words “real past paper” does not create redistribution rights.

If a learner names a book but cannot supply a licensed list, use the book only as inspiration for scale or themes. Build from independently curated, official, open, or licensed sources and state that clearly.

## Accuracy and review

- Keep answer keys, word limits, acceptable variants, question numbering, audio locators, and source locators in structured data.
- Review each generated question for one defensible answer and plausible but non-ambiguous distractors.
- Use official band descriptors as evaluation dimensions when permitted, but describe AI output as practice feedback or an estimate—not an official IELTS score.
- Keep an audit trail of source URL, retrieval date, license or permission basis, transformation, and reviewer.
- Avoid copying long passages into documentation or prompts when a source link and structured locator are sufficient.

## Product wording

Use precise labels such as “原创 IELTS 风格练习” or “IELTS 官方公开样题”. Avoid “官方真题”, “真实原卷”, “官方评分”, or affiliation claims unless evidence and permission support them.

Before a public or app-store release, review branding, trademarks, privacy, recorded voice rights, content licenses, and any official-looking UI that could imply endorsement.
