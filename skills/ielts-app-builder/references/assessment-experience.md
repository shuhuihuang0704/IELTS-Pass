# Assessment experience

Use this reference for listening, reading, speaking, writing, audio, answer cards, transcripts, feedback, retries, and question notes.

## Shared assessment shell

On wide screens, keep source material and the answer workspace side by side with independent scrolling. The learner should not need to scroll away from the answer card to find the passage, prompt, chart, or audio controls.

On narrow screens, stack the material and answer area in a predictable order, keep progress and submission reachable, and preserve the current question when switching context.

An exam unit needs stable question numbering, nearby inputs, saved draft state, a clear submit action, a neutral retry state, and post-submit review. Do not repeat question numbers or start a full test midway.

## Listening

- Use the relevant IELTS families: form/note/table/summary completion, multiple choice, multiple selection, matching, map/plan/diagram labelling, and sentence or short-answer completion.
- Provide native pause/resume and seek controls. “Replay” must not be the only way to continue after pausing.
- Keep the transcript hidden until the current task is submitted. Retrying re-locks it for the new attempt while preserving old review history.
- Correct answers need no generic improvement warning. Wrong-answer feedback should distinguish spelling, singular/plural, word-limit, distractor, missed synonym, and lost-position errors.
- A dictation batch may play several words or phrases in one track with deliberate gaps. Let the learner pause and seek; avoid a slow one-word-next-button loop.
- Dialogue voices should be audibly distinct. Preprocess spelling, numbers, abbreviations, names, and punctuation so text-to-speech does not sound electronic or malformed.
- Never synthesise labels such as “Question 1 answer and confusing context” into learner audio.

## Reading

- Support matching headings, matching information/features, multiple choice, TRUE/FALSE/NOT GIVEN, YES/NO/NOT GIVEN, sentence completion, summary/table/flow-chart completion, and short answer as appropriate.
- Render highlights inside the source passage, not in a duplicate excerpt above it.
- Explanations should identify passage, paragraph, and a stable sentence or line locator, then explain the paraphrase and why distractors fail.
- NOT GIVEN explanations must state what was checked and what evidence is absent; do not invent a supporting sentence.
- For a full Academic Reading simulation, preserve the 1–40 sequence across three independent passages while keeping each passage's own answers and submission state isolated.

## Speaking

- The examiner asks by audio only after the learner starts the session. Offer pause/resume and optional captions.
- Part 1 is short personal questions. Part 2 normally includes one minute of preparation and a longer turn. Part 3 is a two-way abstract discussion.
- If the app adds preparation time to Part 1 or Part 3, label it as training assistance rather than authentic exam timing.
- Follow-up questions should use the learner's preceding answer. Fall back to a prepared route only when the answer contains too little usable content.
- Show pronunciation, fluency, grammar, vocabulary, and topic-structure feedback after the relevant response or completed set—not before the learner answers.
- Keep recording permission errors, unsupported browsers, transcription failure, and empty audio recoverable. Never mark a silent or missing recording complete.

## Writing

- Keep the prompt/chart visible beside a large editor on desktop.
- Save drafts without counting them as submitted.
- Enforce the appropriate task minimum only when the product intentionally requires a complete submission; otherwise make the practice mode explicit.
- Post-submit feedback should quote or highlight the learner's text, distinguish strengths from changes, give a reason, provide a concrete rewrite, and suggest missing comparisons, examples, counterarguments, or development.
- A model answer must be readable digital text and remain hidden until the intended review stage.

## Audio attached to notes

Store question-to-audio locators rather than copying a whole test recording into every note. Include a short lead-in and tail so the learner hears the answer plus nearby competing information. Clamp the window to the source track and retain the source ID for future re-generation.
